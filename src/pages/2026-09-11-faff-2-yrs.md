Today marks ~2 years of faff. We thought of a product we thought we’d enjoy using ourselves, asked ourselves and other very smart people why it did not exist, and got mostly bad answers. So we thought we’d find out for ourselves. Turns out the smart people were right on many counts and wrong on some (idk, the jury is still out). But I don’t want to talk about that right now. Or why this business of solving hard technical and operational problems speaks to me more than anything else I could have worked on. I want to tell y’all about why 2 years is a big deal to me personally and something I’d like to celebrate more than any milestone, metric, or a fundraise. 

The tl;dr is simple: it is two years of not dying. It is common startup gyaan from the aforementioned smart people that startups are “default dead”. I thought I knew that meant that the company *might* fail. I think it really just means that every week you have to manufacture a reason for the company to continue existing. Or maybe it’s a secret third thing I haven’t discovered yet.

The last two years have been hard and have tested (and broken) me in ways I never thought possible. Rewards are scarce (but overwhelming), there are no external kudos, and there are always N fires to put out. But I think that is exactly what I wanted out of this when I decided to start up even if I didn’t know it back then - a problem hard enough that suffering is unavoidable. I am very grateful to Anirudh, the team, our early supporters, and all of y’all for helping me get through it in whatever way you did, even when I couldn’t myself. In hindsight, that is more than I could have ever asked for - a chance to ENDURE AND SURVIVE (albeit while whining and complaining). 

“endure and survive, for what?”, I often get asked - by friends and family who are (rightly) sick of my whining, other people looking to start up themselves, and potential investors / employees hoping for a big grand vision. I honestly don’t know. Maybe because I see a promised land at the end of the tunnel. Or maybe simply because it is fun (on most days anyway). But mostly, for the itch of it. I was never the big-picture-zoom-out-5-year-roadmap type of person anyway. 

Cheers to not dying and hopefully having fun along the way :)
